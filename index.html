The Gap

Before we started building, we went looking for what already existed. Here's what we found.

01

The Filipino professional paradox

Filipino-Canadians are the most educated (0 bachelor's+), most employed (0), and most overqualified (0) racialized group in Canada. The talent is there. The system doesn't reward it proportionally: degree-holders earn 0 less than non-racialized peers. Root cause: credential non-recognition compounded by historical immigration pathways. For Filipino women who entered via the caregiver pathway, 76% are overqualified. Those who break through to VP+ share an experience almost no one around them understands.

2021 Census, Statistics Canada, Catalogue 98-200-X, issue 2021011; 2024 Labour Force Survey, Catalogue 14-28-0001-X (published August 2025)
02

40+ Filipino organizations. Zero at the executive level.

The gap isn't in Filipino organizations broadly. It's specifically at the executive tier. Every Filipino org is either industry-specific (nurses, accountants, engineers), SMB-focused (business chambers), or youth-oriented (Rise Tribe). The cross-industry, senior-level peer connection that other communities have doesn't exist for Filipino leaders.

Landscape scan of 40+ Filipino organizations in the GTA, March 2026
03

Pan-Asian networks don't fill the gap

Ascend Canada and NAAAP serve the broader Pan-Asian community, and Filipinos can and do participate. But Filipino leaders face distinctive challenges: caregiver pathway stigma, the highest overqualification rate of any racialized group, and cultural factors around leadership self-advocacy that differ from other Asian communities. Every other major Asian diaspora group has built their own community-specific executive infrastructure AND participates in pan-Asian networks. Both coexist. The Filipino community has neither.

Comparative analysis of South Asian, Chinese-Canadian, and Filipino executive infrastructure, March 2026
04

Most Filipino senior leaders are invisible

ACCE celebrates Chinese-Canadian entrepreneurs with an 800-guest gala sponsored by all five major Canadian banks. ICCC recognizes Indo-Canadian achievement at its awards night. Nothing equivalent exists for Filipino professionals. There are notable exceptions, but no collective visibility infrastructure. Most Filipino senior leaders are invisible to each other, to corporate Canada, and to the next generation.

ACCE 28th Annual Awards Gala (2025); ICCC est. 1977, 1,000+ members
05

Mentorship flows down. Lateral support and board pipelines don't exist.

Rise Tribe mentors youth. IFCNA mentors nurses through licensing. But there is no space where senior Filipino leaders support each other as peers. CCBC's board includes a Power Corp executive. ACCE galas draw senators and mayors. Filipino senior leaders have no collective platform from which to access board opportunities or political networks.

Rise Tribe (est. 2016); IFCNA (est. 2019, 4,500+ members); CCBC (est. 1978)

To put this in perspective:

South Asian senior leaders in the GTA have a chamber of commerce that runs trade missions to India, a global entrepreneurship network with 15,000+ members where successful founders mentor emerging ones while connecting with each other as peers, and awards programs that make their achievement visible to corporate Canada and government.

Indo Canada Chamber of Commerce (est. 1977, 1,000+ members, 7 national chapters); TiE Toronto (15,000+ global network, Charter Member tier at $500+/year)

Chinese-Canadian senior leaders have a business council whose board chair is an SVP at Power Corporation and whose executive director testifies before parliamentary committees. They have an annual awards gala that draws 800 guests and is sponsored by all five major Canadian banks. Their business associations co-present festivals that draw 80,000 people and have formalized collective advocacy across the GTA.

Canada China Business Council (est. 1978, 7 offices); ACCE (est. 1994, 28th annual gala 2025); Toronto Chinese Business Association (est. 1972)

Filipino senior leaders have none of this.
No peer network. No corporate sponsorship. No collective visibility.
No board pipeline. No political access.

These findings are drawn from a comprehensive landscape scan covering 40+ Filipino organizations, demographic data from Statistics Canada (2021 Census and 2024 Labour Force Survey), and a comparative analysis of executive infrastructure in the Chinese-Canadian and South Asian communities in the Greater Toronto Area.

What We're Building

Instead of looking for these spaces, we're creating one.

A peer community for Filipino and Filipina senior leaders in the Toronto/GTA. VP+, C-suite, board members, and founders running meaningful businesses. The community's purpose isn't pre-decided. We're bringing the right people together and letting what this becomes emerge from the room.

A room for Filipino leaders who've built something significant, whether that meant navigating the bamboo ceiling, being the only Filipino at the leadership table, or carrying the invisible weight of representing your community whether you signed up for it or not.

01

Lateral support at the top

The Filipino community has no shortage of people giving back. But most of that energy flows downward. What's missing is peers who understand what it takes to lead at this level.

02

Celebrating Filipino leadership

A community where being Filipino is a strength, not something to code-switch around.

03

Broader influence, over time

If we get this right, it could lead to something bigger: pathways to board seats and real influence, so we're not just supporting each other but reshaping the systems we operate in.

Who It's For

Filipino and Filipina leaders at the VP+, C-suite, board, and founder level in the Toronto/GTA. Cross-industry by design. All genders. The kind of room where everyone in it shares the experience of leading at this level.

We're not looking for attendees. We're looking for the people whose perspective will define what this community becomes.
